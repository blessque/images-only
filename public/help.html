justimages

How this site works

Everything is done from the site itself. You never need a terminal, a code editor, or the Cloudflare dashboard for ordinary work.

Getting into admin mode

Press Option + \, or click the small grey lock at the very bottom of the page. Type your password.

Admin mode switches off whenever you reload the page. That is deliberate — it means you can never leave the site unlocked by accident.

Adding photographs

Drag files anywhere onto the page, or press ⌘ + I. They land in a tray at the bottom where you can arrange them before publishing.

  • Your photographs are shrunk on your own computer, before they upload. A 10MB file usually leaves as about 600KB.
  • The tray shows the real before and after sizes, so you can judge it yourself.
  • Drag rows in the tray to set the order. That is the order they appear on the site.
  • Small files, under about 150KB, are uploaded untouched — there is nothing to gain by re-compressing them.

High fidelity on a photograph keeps more detail at the cost of a bigger file. Use it where an image really needs it, not everywhere.

Arranging the grid

Click any photograph to get its controls. Then ← and →, or the arrows on the bar, move it.

Each photograph has a size class. These do not change how big it is — they change how many share a row:

  • solo — takes a whole row by itself.
  • wide — shares a row with one or two others.
  • tight — packs in with more.
Photographs are never cropped, ever. The row height changes instead. That is why two images with the same shape look the same width even on different size classes — inside one row, everything shares a height.

Text on the page

In admin mode the name and contact line at the bottom become editable. Click and type. That is the only text on the whole site.

Alt text describes a photograph for people using a screen reader, and for search engines. It is filled in from the filename and you can improve it. A camera filename like IMG_4821 is left empty rather than used as nonsense.

Deleting

Delete shows an Undo button for a few seconds. Even after that, a deleted photograph is kept for 30 days rather than destroyed — but getting it back after the undo disappears needs a developer, so use Undo while it is there.

Everything saves by itself. There is no Save button and nothing to forget.

Backing everything up

Download everything in the admin bar gives you a zip of every photograph plus a list of them. Do this occasionally and keep it somewhere else.

That zip is not just a backup — it is what a developer would use to move this site to another host entirely, without losing anything.

If you forget your password

It cannot be recovered — the site only stores a scrambled version that cannot be turned back. It can be replaced, in about a minute:

  • Sign in at dash.cloudflare.com
  • Storage & Databases → D1 → justimages → Console
  • Run: DELETE FROM auth;
  • Reload your site. It will ask you to choose a new password.
While the password is deleted, anyone who opens the site can claim it. Do these steps one after another, not hours apart.

After several wrong attempts the site stops accepting any password for 15 minutes, including the right one. That is protection against someone guessing. Wait it out rather than trying harder.

Limits worth knowing

  • About 600 photographs of storage on the free plan.
  • Around 250 photographs a day can be uploaded. The tray warns you before a batch is too big — splitting it across two days costs nothing.
  • A newly published photograph can take up to a minute to appear everywhere. If one looks broken immediately after uploading, wait a minute and reload.

Videos and GIFs

Small animated GIFs work. Large ones lose their animation, and video is not supported — it needs a different kind of hosting. Link to YouTube or Vimeo instead, or ask a developer.

If something looks broken

  • Reload the page first. Admin mode switches off, which is normal.
  • If images are missing right after uploading, wait a minute.
  • If the site will not accept your password, check you are not locked out from repeated attempts, then use the reset steps above.
  • If the whole site is down, it is the host, not something you did. Your photographs are safe.
